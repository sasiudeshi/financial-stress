# Financial Weather — User Guide

Financial Weather is a quick check-in on your spending. You answer a few
questions about your income and how you spend it, and it gives you a
gentle read on how things currently look: **Clear**, **Changeable**, or
**Stormy** — like a weather forecast for your finances, not a report card.

Nothing you enter is stored or shared. Every time you use it, it's a
fresh, private check.

## What the form is asking

The form is split into five short sections. Here's what each question means:

### About you
- **Age** — your age in years.
- **Employment status** — Employed, Self-employed, Student, or Unemployed.
- **Dependents** — how many people rely on you financially (children,
  family members you support, etc.).

### Income & expenditure
- **Monthly income** — your total income in a typical month.
- **Monthly expenditure** — everything you spend in a typical month, added up.
- **Essential expenditure** — the part of your spending that goes to
  necessities: housing, food, bills, healthcare.
- **Non-essential expenditure** — the part that goes to things you could
  live without: entertainment, shopping, eating out, etc.
- **Planned expenditure** — what you *intended* to spend this month, before
  the month actually happened. (It's fine if this doesn't match what you
  actually spent — that gap is useful information, not a mistake.)

### Category breakdown
A rough split of your monthly spending across ten everyday categories:
housing, utilities, food, healthcare, education, transportation, shopping,
entertainment, subscriptions, and other. These don't need to add up
perfectly to your total monthly expenditure — ballpark figures are fine.

### Debt & savings
- **Debt repayment** — what you pay monthly across all loans and debts combined.
- **Financial commitments** — how many active loans or financial
  commitments you're currently juggling.
- **Savings this month** — how much you set aside this month. If you spent
  more than you earned, this can be a **negative** number — that's a normal,
  valid answer, not an error.

### Spending behavior
- **Transactions per month** — roughly how many purchases/payments you make in a month.
- **Spending volatility** — how much your spending tends to swing from one
  month to the next (a rough sense of "steady" vs. "unpredictable").
- **Impulse purchases** — purchases you made on the spur of the moment, per month.
- **Online purchases** — purchases made online, per month.
- **Unplanned expenses** — expenses that came up unexpectedly, per month.
- **Budget exceeded (last 3 months)** — how many of the last 3 months you
  went over budget (0 to 3).
- **Can you save regularly?** — Yes, Sometimes, or No.
- **Balance checks per week** — how often you check your account balance
  in a typical week.
- **Spending change during hard periods** — on a scale of 1 to 5, how your
  spending changes when money is tight: 1 means you spend much *less* than
  usual, 5 means you spend much *more* than usual.

## Reading your result

After you submit, you'll see one of three "weather" states:

- **Clear (Low stress)** — your finances currently look steady.
- **Changeable (Moderate stress)** — there are some ups and downs worth
  keeping an eye on, but nothing alarming.
- **Stormy (High stress)** — things look turbulent right now. This is meant
  as a nudge to pay closer attention, not a verdict — storms pass, and
  knowing the forecast is what lets you plan around it.

### Confidence breakdown

Underneath the result, you'll see three bars — one for each of Clear,
Changeable, and Stormy — with a percentage next to each. This shows how
confident the system is in each possibility. For example, if you see
"Stormy 70%, Changeable 25%, Clear 5%", it means the system leans fairly
strongly toward Stormy, but it isn't 100% certain — Changeable is a real
possibility too. A very lopsided result (like 95% for one state) means the
system is quite confident; a closer split means your situation sits nearer
a boundary between two states.

### "What stood out" (contributing factors)

This lists the 2–3 things about your answers that most influenced the
result — in plain language, like "High debt-to-income ratio" or "Low
savings ratio". These aren't a full explanation of your finances, just the
handful of factors that mattered most *for this particular check*. If you
change those specific answers and check again, you'd likely see a
different result.

### Suggestions

A short list of general, gentle pointers related to what stood out — things
like "Consider reviewing recurring debt obligations relative to income."
These are meant as starting points for your own thinking, not instructions.

## Important: what this is, and isn't

**This is an automated estimate based on spending patterns, not a
financial or psychological diagnosis.** It's built from a machine learning
model trained on data, and like any estimate, it can be wrong. It does not
know your full financial picture, your goals, or your circumstances.

It is **not** professional financial advice, and it is **not** a mental
health assessment. If you're dealing with real financial difficulty or
distress, please talk to a qualified financial advisor, a debt counseling
service, or — if it's affecting your wellbeing — a healthcare professional.
This tool is meant to prompt useful reflection, nothing more.
